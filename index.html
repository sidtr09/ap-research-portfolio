02Process

From question to method

This section explains my research question, why it matters, the research I built on, and how I set up my experiment.

My research question
If a self-driving RC car only gets a fixed amount of real-world practice, will it learn to race faster and use fewer resources if it practices on the tracks with the biggest instead of on random tracks?
Hypothesis

If the car practices on the tracks where the simulator is most wrong, then it will learn to finish laps on new tracks using fewer real-world and with fewer crashes than a car that practices on randomly chosen tracks.

Where the idea came from

Ideation
In the simulator
∞steps

In a simulator, a agent can practice for millions of steps overnight. Crashes cost nothing, and the car resets instantly.

On the real car
~30min

On the real car, every step uses battery, crashes can break parts, and I have to reset the car by hand. My whole budget is about 30 minutes of driving.

The gap in research
?

Most methods assume practice is cheap or unlimited. I wanted to know how a robot should choose what to practice when real practice is limited.

Design decision

Why does the car have two power systems?

The drive motor pulls sudden bursts of current, which creates electrical noise. If the Pi shared that power, a hard acceleration could make it lose power and restart in the middle of a lap. To prevent this, the Pi has its own voltage regulator (a ) with a 5 A fuse. The and ESP32 get power from the 's built-in regulator. The two systems only share a common ground.

Why this matters

In the simulator, the car needed about 33,000 steps just to finish one lap. On the real car, I only get 15,000 steps in total. That means the car can't learn from scratch in the real world. Every real step has to count, which is why I want to spend them where the simulator is most wrong.

05Perspective

Why it matters

What I'm learning, why this project matters to me, and who it could help. Some parts are not completed yet.

Inspiration

Racing at the limit

Race cars drive at the edge of grip, where small mistakes turn into crashes. That makes racing a tough test. If a car can learn to race safely with very little real practice, the same idea could help other robots learn safely too.

Impact potential

Where real practice is expensive

Some robots can't afford to crash while they learn. Choosing what to practice more carefully could help them learn with less risk and cost.

Safety

ISEF rules

Emergency stop

Pressing the spacebar on my computer sends a wireless signal that stops the steering and motor right away.

Battery safety

I charge and store the battery in a fire-resistant bag, never leave it charging alone, and use a low-voltage alarm.

Supervised testing

I test in a closed area with no bystanders. An adult sponsor supervises building, battery work, and fast test runs.

Soldering and disposal

I wear eye protection and use a fume fan when soldering. A damaged battery gets fully drained and taken to a county e-waste site.
